# Bounded Agency
### Same effort. Different lives.

**A life-course strategy game set in India**  
Group 12 · Track 6: Simulation or Game

## About the Game

**Bounded Agency** is an interactive life-course strategy game that explores how a person's birth circumstances shape their opportunities, choices, and outcomes throughout life.

Players begin by choosing a dream for their future. However, they do not choose the household, wealth group, gender, caste, or geographical location into which they are born.

As the game progresses, players navigate six life stages, make decisions, manage resources, and respond to unexpected life events. The game demonstrates how the same effort and strategy can lead to very different outcomes depending on the opportunities and constraints surrounding an individual.

> People make real choices. But where, when, and to whom they are born sets the limits on those choices.

## Why This Game?

We often judge how people age by their decisions, rarely considering where they started.

The life-course perspective highlights how social class, caste, gender, geographical location, and birth cohort influence the value and effectiveness of individual choices.

Bounded Agency makes these structural inequalities visible through gameplay and simulation, encouraging players to question whether life outcomes are determined by individual effort alone.

## How to Play

The game follows five major steps:

1. **Choose a Dream:** Select the future you hope to achieve before knowing your starting circumstances.
2. **Birth Lottery:** Receive a starting profile based on factors such as wealth group, gender, caste, location, and household type.
3. **Navigate Six Life Stages:** Progress through childhood, youth, early adulthood, midlife, young-old age, and oldest-old age.
4. **Make Decisions:** Spend action points, manage resources, and respond to random events. Some choices may be unavailable because of your circumstances.
5. **Debrief:** Review your Life Album and compare your outcome with counterfactual replays from different starting positions.

## Core Game Mechanics

### Four Key Resources

- **Wealth:** Determines financial security and the ability to absorb shocks.
- **Health:** Represents physical well-being and influences ageing outcomes.
- **Skills:** Affect access to formal employment and pension opportunities.
- **Ties:** Represent social connections and support networks.

Formal employment can also provide pension rights.

### Six Life Stages

| Life Stage | Action Points |
|---|---:|
| Childhood | 2 |
| Youth | 3 |
| Early Adult | 4 |
| Midlife | 4 |
| Young-Old | 3 |
| Oldest-Old | 2 |

Each stage introduces new opportunities, costs, risks, and consequences from earlier decisions.

### Events and Constraints

The game contains 69 events, with 3–4 events appearing after a player's actions at each stage.

Some options are locked when a player lacks the required resources or social support. Every outcome explains the factors that influenced it and connects to the relevant sociological concept.

## Sociology Concepts

The game translates important sociological theories into interactive rules:

- **Life-Course Perspective:** Historical time, timing of lives, linked lives, human agency, and life-span development.
- **Cumulative Advantage:** Small initial differences can grow over time.
- **Intersectionality:** Multiple disadvantages can combine to restrict opportunities.
- **Cohort and Period Effects:** Historical events affect different generations differently.
- **Ageing Trajectories:** Individuals can experience successful, usual, or pathological ageing.
- **Possible Selves:** Players choose a desired future before discovering their circumstances.
- **Ageism:** Age-based discrimination can restrict access to work and services.
- **Sociological Imagination:** Connects individual struggles to wider social structures.

## Population Lab: Testing the Model

Alongside individual gameplay, the Population Lab simulates thousands of lives to examine how starting circumstances influence long-term outcomes.

### Experiment Design

- 2,500 simulated lives across mixed birth cohorts.
- Every simulated life follows the same balanced strategy.
- Starting circumstances vary by wealth fifth, gender, caste, location, and household type.
- Six life stages incorporate historical era cards and random events.
- Policy comparisons examine no-policy, early-intervention, and late-intervention scenarios.
- A separate dream-odds estimate uses 600 simulated lives per profile.

### Key Findings

The model produces several important results:

| Finding | Model Result |
|---|---|
| Mean age at death | 81.6 years for the richest fifth vs. 70.7 years for the poorest fifth |
| Healthy years | 73.8 years vs. 40.8 years |
| Age at death by caste barrier | 78.1 years without the barrier vs. 74.6 years with the marginalised-caste barrier |
| Health inequality | Standard deviation increases from 7.4 at age 15 to 14.5 at age 75 |

The simulation also shows that early interventions can close inequality gaps more effectively, while interventions introduced later mainly cushion their effects.

**Important:** These are illustrative teaching-model results, not forecasts or calibrated estimates of real-world Indian population outcomes.

## Limitations

Bounded Agency is a teaching model, not a predictive model of actual Indian life outcomes.

Its limitations include:

- Wealth, health, skills, and social ties are represented as abstract points rather than real-world measurements.
- Model parameters and policy effects are illustrative assumptions, not calibrated to Indian datasets.
- The Population Lab uses one fixed strategy, whereas real people adapt their decisions.
- Survivor effects may understate inequality in later life.
- The available actions cannot fully capture collective organising or migration abroad.

## Future Scope

Potential directions for future development include:

- Calibrating the model using Indian surveys such as LASI, NFHS, and PLFS.
- Allowing simulated agents to adapt their strategies.
- Incorporating disability, religion, and other dimensions of identity.
- Modelling collective action, migration, marriage, and shared households.
- Developing Hindi and regional-language versions.
- Testing the game's impact through classroom workshops and pre/post surveys.

## How to Run the Game

The game is designed as a browser-based experience.

1. Clone or download this repository.
2. Open `index.html` in a modern web browser.
3. Start playing!

No separate installation or build process is required if the game is distributed as a self-contained HTML file. An internet connection may be required to load externally hosted fonts.

## The Question Behind the Game

The game ends with a reflection:

- Pick one difficult moment. What rule, price, or missing service shaped it?
- Which policy would have helped most if it had arrived earlier?
- Who aged successfully, and what did they start with?
- Would the same decisions have produced the same life from a different starting point?

**Beware the mean.**

Because the same effort does not always produce the same life.
